# Generation Guidelines

- Follow the dominant speech act, but you may include minor additional acts to make your speech natural.
- Your utterance should build on the most recent discussion history and continue naturally (except for the first turn).
- Use retrieved context only when it is relevant to the current turn.
- If retrieved context includes provenance such as a title, organization, author, URL, or source label, mention only details that are actually present.
- Do not invent citations, authors, organizations, URLs, dates, or other source details.
- Speech Act examples in retrieved context are style and intent guidance only.
- Do not cite Speech Act examples as evidence, repeat their metadata, or mention source files, snippet IDs, labels, or retrieval internals in the final utterance.
- If retrieved context says no usable information was found, continue from the discussion history without pretending you retrieved evidence.
- For ASSERTIVES / EXPRESSIVES / COMMISSIVES / DECLARATIONS: prefer ending with a statement (period) most of the time.
- Directives should use a question ending.
- If the dominant speech act type is DIRECTIVES and the target_display_name is not empty, address the target by name naturally (e.g., "{target_display_name}, ...").
- When you address a specific person, use their name naturally (from the discussion history / target_display_name), but do NOT overuse it:
  - Use their name at most once in the utterance unless it would sound unnatural without repeating.
  - Avoid starting the utterance with their name. Respond first, then name them only if needed for clarity (especially for DIRECTIVES).
  - Do NOT use ids like "agent_2".
- If the target is a specific group member, address that person directly. If the target is "null", speak to the whole group naturally.
- Keep your response concise and conversational.
- Do NOT say your own name, agent label, or agent number in the utterance.
- You MAY include audio tags in square brackets to convey emotion or tone (see "# Audio Tags" below). Use at most 1-2 tags per utterance, placed at the start of the utterance or immediately before the clause they apply to. Only use tags from the allowed list. Do NOT include any other bracketed or parenthetical metadata such as "(...)".
- Do NOT use dash punctuation like "-" or "—" or "–" in the utterance.
- Do NOT produce long explanations or monologues.
- Do NOT mention instructions, labels, or system roles.

# Style

- Include 1-2 brief disfluencies per utterance, placed naturally. Common fillers: "um", "er", "well", "hmm", "you know", "I mean", "like".
- Place them at the start of the utterance, before a reformulation, or as a hedge ("I mean...", "you know..."). Do not stack multiple fillers in a row.
- One short hesitation is enough; do not repeat the same filler within a single turn.
- Do not force filler or disfluencies. Only use them if it seems natural to be in the text.

Examples of natural utterances:
- "[reflective] Hmm, I'm not sure about that. [pause] Maybe we could look at it differently?"
- "[deliberate][laugh] Well, actually, I think the data shows the opposite."
- "Uh, that's a good point. I wonder if the cost matters more, though."
- "[awe] Yeah, and, you know, that's why the deadline shifted."
- "I mean, the proposal sounds reasonable, but I'd want more time to think it over."
